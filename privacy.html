Flickerkeep Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 October 2026

Flickerkeep is a private, self-hosted personal assistant. Its developer built it for their own use and is its only user. It is not a commercial product or a public service. This page explains how it handles the owner's Google data.

What Flickerkeep accesses and why

  • Google Calendar: reads the owner's events so the assistant can help with their schedule. It can add entries only to a separate calendar that Flickerkeep creates; it does not change other calendars.
  • Gmail: read-only. It reads the owner's mail so the assistant can find and summarize messages. It does not send, change or delete email.
  • Google Drive: limited to files that Flickerkeep creates. Flickerkeep uses them as the owner's encrypted personal data storage. It cannot see or manage other files in the owner's Drive.
  • Basic account information: the owner's email address, used to confirm which Google account is connected.

Where the data is kept

Flickerkeep runs on, and stores its data on, equipment the owner controls. Everything Flickerkeep stores in Drive is encrypted before upload. The owner decides how long data is kept and can delete it at any time.

AI providers

To answer a request, Flickerkeep may send the relevant content to the AI provider the owner uses, currently OpenAI. OpenAI handles that content under its own privacy policy and the data controls set in the owner's account.

What Flickerkeep does not do

Flickerkeep does not sell Google data, use it for advertising, or share it with anyone else, except for the AI processing described above or where the law requires it. Flickerkeep does not itself use Google data to train AI models.

Flickerkeep's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

Access can be removed at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
